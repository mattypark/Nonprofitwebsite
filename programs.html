Axiom/Programs

Two divisions, one loop.

Most high school programs stop at the paper. Most internships never touch research. Axiom runs both — a Research Division that publishes, and a Startup Division that ships — and wires them together so findings become products and problems become questions.

Cohort length8 weeks Team size3–5 students OutputPaper + artifact
— The two divisions

Research that ships. Internships that think.

Every student picks a division. Teams are small. Output is real. The divisions aren't walled off — they trade questions and answers across the cohort.

01 — Research Division

Teams that publish and build.

Students work in teams of 3–5 on focused AI/ML questions, guided by a mentor and a clear publication target. Every research project is paired with a follow-through: a prototype, a tool, a model, or an open-source repo. If you publish a paper with Axiom, you ship something with it.

  • Focused topics in AI, ML, and CS — chosen by the team
  • Mentor-guided weekly syncs and milestone reviews
  • Output: a peer-reviewed paper plus an applied artifact
  • Published on the Axiom Journal — a mini arXiv for high school research
Paper + artifact · 8-week sprints
02 — Startup Division

Embedded with real startups.

Students get placed with partner startups to work on a specific scoped project — as an intern, shadow, or embedded contributor. Real product work, real engineering, real problems. Think of it as a structured way for high schoolers to get their hands on the work that usually requires being a college junior with a network.

  • Scoped project inside an early- or growth-stage startup
  • Mentorship directly from founders and engineers
  • Output: shipped work — a feature, a tool, a research spike, an internal model
  • Matched by interest, not connections
Embedded · 8-week placements
— Cross-division collaboration

Research → application → new questions → more research.

Research teams surface findings that startup teams can apply to a partner's problem. Startup teams surface real-world problems that research teams can dig into. The loop is the point — high schoolers get both the depth and the ship-it experience.
